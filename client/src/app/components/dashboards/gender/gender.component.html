The works. Every record whose type is a school, academy, college, seminary, asylum, home, hospital, infirmary, convent or other religious house; parishes, chapels and stations are left out. Each institution counts once a year.

Who ran it. Read from the record's name, type and note: a women's congregation named ("under the charge of the Sisters of Charity", "Ursuline Convent"), men religious ("the Christian Brothers", "Jesuits"), or lay teachers. A convent with no men named is counted as a women's house. Where the almanac says nothing, the clergy listed there stand in: men religious if their names carry an order (S.J., C.SS.R.), else diocesan clergy. "Women and men religious" are schools split between them, "boys under the Christian Brothers, girls under the Sisters of St. Joseph". "Sisters, not named" are works the almanac gives to sisters, nuns or a convent without naming the congregation.

Whom it served. Girls ("Female Orphan Asylum", "for young ladies"), boys ("Male Parish School", "for boys") or both. Most records don't say, and the 1830s almanacs say least of all.

What this cannot show. The almanacs list clergy by name, and almost never women: sisters appear as congregations running institutions, not as people. The counts are of institutions, not of the sisters, brothers or pupils in them, and a work the almanac listed without a note counts as "not stated" however it was run. These markers are read from every record each time the data is loaded, so records added later are read the same way; notes in wording the patterns don't know are listed on the Data Issues page.
