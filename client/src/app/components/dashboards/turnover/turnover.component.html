An institution is "newly listed" in a year when the almanac lists it and did not the year before, and "no longer listed" the other way round. A record can be missing only because its diocese wasn't transcribed that year, so each comparison counts only dioceses with records in both years, and only within a run of consecutive almanacs (1833–38, 1864–70). Newly listed is not the same as newly founded: the almanacs also added places as their reporting improved.

"How long new institutions lasted" follows those first listed in 1865 or later (so not merely the first almanac of a run), and asks whether each is listed again one, two … five years on, counting only years its diocese was transcribed. Points resting on fewer than 20 institutions, or fewer than 15 priests at posts, are left out of the charts, so a single small diocese may show only part of a line. "Listed again in 1864" matches institution ids across the 1838–1864 gap.

A priest "left his post" when the almanac listed him as resident at an institution one year and not the next. Some apparent departures are one person recorded under two ids; the Data Issues page lists those to review. A stay counts as complete only when its first and last years both fall inside a run with the years around them transcribed; the table gives each diocese's average, since most stays seen whole last a single year.
