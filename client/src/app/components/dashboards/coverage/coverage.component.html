Each grid cell counts the distinct institutions recorded for a diocese in one almanac year, on a square-root scale so the largest sees don’t flatten the rest. A light cell means the diocese existed but has no records encoded yet; a hatched cell means it did not exist that year (from a timeline of when each see was founded).

Dioceses are keyed by the prefix of their institutions’ IDs, so name changes (e.g. “New York” / “New York City”) are merged onto one row under the latest name.

The pink band between 1861 and 1864 marks 1862–63: the Catholic Almanac was not published during those Civil War years. Individual pink cells mark other see-years whose records are absent or disrupted; hover one for the reason.

The map bins the same records onto a grid that refines as you zoom in, with shading scaled to the cells in view.
