Every record carries a language, "English" unless the almanac said otherwise, so English is the background here, not a finding. The almanac's own words in that column ("German", "French and Menomony", "Sermon in French and occasionally English") are split into each language they name; a church marked "German and English" counts as German.

Some congregations are marked only in their name or note: "German Church", "a large German congregation", "Indian Mission", "for colored children". Those are read with narrow phrases (never "Indiana", nor nations whose names are also places, such as Ottawa or Sioux), and the map says when a mark came from a note. In all, {{ sources.listed | number }} records are marked in the language column and {{ sources.note | number }} in a name or note.

"Native American" gathers missions the almanac called Indian and those it named by nation (Chippewa, Potawatomi, Menominee, Winnebago, Penobscot, Passamaquoddy, Papago, Navajo). "Black congregations" gathers the almanac's "African" and its churches, chapels and schools "for colored people" or "children". These are the almanac's words, kept so the records can be found; they mark who a place served, not a language.

The almanac marked a language unevenly: some dioceses noted every German parish, others none, and the 1830s volumes say little. Counts are a floor. The CSV lists the year's records with where each mark was found.
