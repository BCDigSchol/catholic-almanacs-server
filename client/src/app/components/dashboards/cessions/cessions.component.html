The cessions are Charles C. Royce's Indian Land Cessions in the United States (Bureau of American Ethnology, 1899), as digitised and revised by the U.S. Forest Service, Office of Tribal Relations: Tribal Lands Ceded to the United States, licensed CC BY 4.0. It maps land the United States took by treaty, act or executive order from 1784 to 1894, so the original states of the east, settled before then, fall outside it.

A cession's date is the day its treaty was signed. Land "still held" in a year is land whose cession was signed after 1 January of that year. Royce's areas often overlap, where several nations ceded the same land or one nation ceded it more than once; a place counts as still held while any of its cessions was yet to come, and otherwise by its latest. Nations are named as Royce's schedule names them; the present-day tribes are the Forest Service's, from the National NAGPRA Program.

The data is for information only and cannot be used to decide title, ownership or legal boundaries. The Forest Service's revisions have not been reviewed by the National NAGPRA Program, and a few cessions have no mapped area yet. "Native American missions" are those the almanacs marked as Indian missions or by nation (see Languages and Congregations).
