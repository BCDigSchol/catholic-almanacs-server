A tie runs from a person to the head of an institution where both were resident in the same almanac year. All under a head reads every record that names exactly one head (pastor, rector, superior, president, prior, abbot, director and the like, and their women's forms; never an "assistant" or "vice-"): everyone else resident there that year served under them. Assistants only is stricter: only people whose own role says assistant, curate or vice-, under that one head, or under the one other resident where no head is named.

Most listings give no role at all (about 25,000 of 33,000), and a record with no named head, or with two, gives no tie. So this is a part of who served under whom, not the whole, and it leans toward colleges and religious communities, whose almanac entries name their officers. Women religious are mostly unnamed, so their lines are nearly absent.

Lines run forward in time. Someone's line is those who served under them, then those who later served under those, and so on; a step up the line must have begun before the step below it. That also keeps communities that rotated their superiors from looping back on themselves. A chain is the longest such run. Two people sharing a name under two ids will break a line, and one id covering two people will join lines that were separate: the Data Issues queues bear on this.

Focus on anyone (search above, a name in a list, or Focus here in a node's details) to see their line as a tree and their neighbourhood in the graph. Dot size reflects PageRank over the arrows, so a head many served under draws large.
