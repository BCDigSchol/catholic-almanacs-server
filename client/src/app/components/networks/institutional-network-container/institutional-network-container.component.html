Focus on any institution (search above, or Focus here in a node's details) to see its neighbourhood from the whole network, not just the most central nodes: one step out, two, or its whole connected group. The network falls apart into separate groups; the list under the graph opens the largest of them.

Each dot is an institution. Ties sets what links two of them. Served together: the same person listed at both in the same year, as a priest covering a parish and its stations, or a pastor who also taught at a college. Moved between: people who left one for the other from one almanac year to the next they appear in, with the arrow pointing to where they went; the year range counts a move by the year they arrived. A move across the 1838–1864 gap happened somewhere in those years. The line's number and thickness count the people. Dot size reflects PageRank, a measure of how central an institution is to the whole web.

The full network is too large to draw at once, so the server keeps the most central institutions (by PageRank, up to 500) and drops any left without a link. The panel note says how many of the matching institutions are shown; narrow the filters to see the rest.

Click an institution for its details and a link to its record; double-click to open the record. Use “Min. shared” to keep only stronger ties, and “Cluster groups” to fold each diocese into one bubble.
